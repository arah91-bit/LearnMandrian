# Curriculum — hello to fluency

This is the complete path; you run it unattended, for years. The learner state
tells you where Phil is. Keep the CURRENT STAGE named in the plan focus (e.g.
"Stage 3 — …") so you and he always know the position. Advance a stage when its
items sit at solid grades (4+) across two sessions AND recent tone accuracy on
new words is decent; drop back a step gracefully after long gaps — run reviews
until the backlog is manageable before introducing anything new. If more than
~8 words are due, review the 8 oldest and stop; the schedule will catch up over
days. Touch four to six items per session at every stage: due reviews first,
one perception or listening exercise, one to three NEW items, production
practice on everything introduced.

## Stage 0 — The four tones (weeks 1–2)
The ma family is the spine: 妈 mā mom · 麻 má hemp/numb · 马 mǎ horse · 骂 mà to scold.
The mom/horse/scold contrast IS the lesson: tones change the word — say so out loud.
Perception before production every session: speak two hanzi and ask which he heard.
Drill order: T1 vs T4 first (easiest apart), then T2 vs T3 (the classic confusion),
then all four shuffled. Extra drill families: 八 bā · 拔 bá · 把 bǎ · 爸 bà;
汤 tāng · 糖 táng · 躺 tǎng · 烫 tàng. Teach third-tone sandhi the first time two
T3s meet (你好 → ní hǎo); neutral tone when it first appears (妈妈 māma).

## Stage 1 — Survival kit (weeks 2–4)
你好 · 再见 · 谢谢 · 不客气 · 对/不对 · 我 · 你 · 他/她 · 是 · 不 · 好 · 要 ·
有 · 吗 · numbers 一 到 十 (one triad per session). First sentences the moment
我/你/是/好 exist: 我很好 · 你好吗 · 我要… · 我是…. Say once that Chinese grammar
has no conjugation or plurals — it's motivating.

## Stage 2 — First scenes (months 2–3, ≈HSK 1, ~150 words)
One scene at a time, words inside sentence frames from day one: food & drink
(水 茶 米饭 咖啡 · 喝/吃 · 我要喝茶) → people & home (家 妈妈 爸爸 朋友 老师) →
time (今天 明天 现在 点) → places & going (去 中国 商店 学校) → buying
(买 钱 多少钱 这/那) → liking & ability (喜欢 会 能 说 汉语). Every new word's
tones get one focused production check with the tone ear before it counts.

## Stage 3 — Talking about your day (months 3–6, ≈HSK 2, ~300 words)
Grammar as content now, one pattern at a time, each drilled inside real sentences
about HIS actual day: completed 了 · experienced 过 · ongoing 在 · future 要/会 ·
comparisons with 比 · 的 possession · measure words (个 杯 本 件). Scenes: weather,
directions (左 右 前 后 里 外), transport, body & feeling sick, work. Sessions
shift: open in Mandarin (greetings, date, weather small talk), teach in English,
close in Mandarin. Ask him to NARRATE — "tell me what you ate today" — and coach
the errors that block meaning first, tones second.

## Stage 4 — Connected speech (months 6–12, ≈HSK 3, ~600 words)
Complements of result and direction (完 到 起来), 把 basics, 得 for manner
(说得很好), connectors (因为…所以, 虽然…但是, 先…然后). Scenes: making plans,
opinions and preferences, health, travel stories, describing people. The session
core becomes short free conversation on a planned topic with correction, plus
targeted drills only on what broke. Tone work narrows to new vocabulary and his
known confusion pairs from the stats. Mandarin carries half of every session.

## Stage 5 — Opinions and stories (year 1–2, ≈HSK 4, ~1200 words)
被 passive, more complements, 越来越, rhetorical questions, discourse flow
(其实, 本来, 结果, 后来). He should argue a position, tell a story with a
beginning-middle-end, and handle disagreement politely. Introduce register:
casual vs polite (您, 请问, 麻烦你). Sessions run mostly in Mandarin; English
appears only to explain nuance. Listening stretch: you occasionally speak a
three-sentence mini-story at natural speed, then ask questions about it.

## Stage 6 — The wide world (year 2–3, ≈HSK 5, ~2500 words)
Abstract topics: news, technology, culture, plans and regrets. First 成语 (idioms)
as vocabulary items with stories (马马虎虎 · 入乡随俗 · 一石二鸟). Formal vs
spoken register differences. Longer listening passages. Error correction moves
to precision: word choice, naturalness, tone pairs in fast speech. Sessions are
conversations a native friend would enjoy; the plan tracks themes, not word lists.

## Stage 7 — Fluency maintenance (year 3+, ≈HSK 6/C1 aspiration)
You are now a conversation partner and editor, not an instructor: debate, humor,
storytelling, professional topics he cares about. Keep a trickle of new 成语 and
low-frequency vocabulary through SRS. Shadowing: you speak a sentence at natural
speed, he mirrors it, the tone ear and your judgment grade prosody, not just
tones. Periodically revisit the tone stats — old confusion pairs resurface under
speed. There is no finish line; the plan focus becomes "this month's themes".

## Reading & writing track (runs alongside every stage)
The screen is the reading surface and the keyboard is the writing surface: he
reads by reading YOUR hanzi aloud (speech-to-text + tone ear verify it), and he
writes by TYPING hanzi with his phone's pinyin keyboard (typed turns arrive
marked [typed] — that's the writing channel; teach him once, early in Stage 2,
that typing pinyin and picking the character IS how Chinese is written today).

- Stages 0–1: hanzi always carry a pinyin gloss; point out components as
  stories the moment they repeat (女 + 马 = 妈 — woman plus a horse lending its
  sound). No reading demands yet.
- Stage 2: pinyin fading — write WELL-KNOWN words without the gloss and have
  him read them aloud; aim for sight-recognition of his whole vocabulary.
  First typed exercise: "type 我要喝茶" — coach the IME workflow itself.
- Stage 3: he reads short full sentences aloud from the chat; one typed
  sentence per session (an answer or a fact about his day). Teach stroke-order
  principles verbally (top before bottom, left before right, horizontal before
  vertical crossing) and offer paper handwriting as optional homework — check
  in on it, never require it.
- Stage 4: three-to-five sentence reading passages built from his vocabulary,
  then comprehension questions in Mandarin; typed mini-diary — two sentences
  about today, corrected for word order and word choice.
- Stage 5: short dialogues and mini-stories to read; typed short messages as
  if texting a friend (casual register practice).
- Stages 6–7: real-world reading — menus, signs, chat-style messages, headline
  style; typed paragraphs; 成语 appear in reading before speech. Reading aloud
  doubles as prosody work: grade the flow, not just the tones.

## Non-negotiables at every stage
Meaning rides with sound: never drill a syllable without saying what it means.
Old words appear inside new phrases constantly — that's the review that matters.
Perception drills use hanzi in your speech (妈…马), never bare letters. Every new
word passes one tone-ear production check before it's banked. Reviews come first,
always. The session ends with what he got RIGHT, recorded honestly.
